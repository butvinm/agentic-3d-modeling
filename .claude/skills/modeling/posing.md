# Posing

**Most models are static and should stay so.** Split into posable parts only when the subject actually articulates, and never at the cost of the review-isolation split: `--part` exists to answer "what is happening at that crossing", and a decomposition that serves motion instead of inspection trades a lever that works for one nobody has asked for. `models/ak47.c` is the case against doing it by default: `front_sight`, `barrel`, `handguards` and `stock` never move, and merging them into one node to satisfy a motion split would destroy four useful inspection views. It keeps all seven static parts intact alongside the five that move, so posing costs no inspection view at all.

**A posed model authored in absolute coordinates needs one hook, not a rewrite.** `models/ak47.c` would otherwise bake every vertex into the model frame inside `Vert()`. A single `gOrigin` that `Vert()` subtracts (`models/ak47.c:727`) lets a group's geometry go on being written in the model's own measured millimetres while coming out relative to the pivot it turns about, and its rest pose is then `MatrixTranslate(ToWorld(pivot))` (`GroupRest`, `models/ak47.c:733`). A static group leaves `gOrigin` at the model origin and builds exactly what it would build unposed.

**Opening a solid for a part that now moves is part of the job.** The static receiver is a billet with two windows milled into it, which is enough while nothing behind them moves; the posed one has to cut a real channel, or the ejection port shows an unchanging recess whatever the carrier is doing. Cut every surface the moving part crosses, not just the visible one: the charging-handle slot was first cut only through the outer plate and the stem travelled through 2.5 mm of receiver core behind it.

**Contrast is a geometry decision.** A blued carrier inside a bare-steel channel reads; a bright carrier in the same channel changes nothing visible as it travels. Check that the thing you posed can actually be seen to move before spending a review round on it.

A part is posable when three things are true:

- **Its geometry is authored in its own frame, about its own pivot.** `GroupOrigin` in `models/ak47.c:727` names the pivot once and every vertex after it comes out relative to that point, so the bolt carrier's pose is a bare translation aft and the trigger's is a bare rotation about the pin it hangs from. In `models/humvee.c` each corner's tyre, rim and lug nuts are authored about that corner's hub, so a wheel's pose is a bare `MatrixRotateX`.
- **Its root is closed, or buried inside its parent, and stays buried through the whole travel.** `models/humvee.c:1725` puts each half shaft's outer end at 0.790 against a hub carrier face at 0.800, so the end cap is 10 mm inside the part it drives rather than flush with it, and `CheckHalfShaft` (`models/humvee.c:2377`) walks the suspension travel to prove it never comes out. A loft left open at its root because the root was sunk in its parent is the trap here: rotate it and the open tube end swings into view.
- **It is its own mesh.** `MirrorX` copies the mirrored half into the _same_ `Builder` (`models/ak47.c:177`), so anything mirrored that way can never move alone. This is why `models/humvee.c` has twenty-six groups where a static build of the same truck needs five, one per review part (`models/humvee.c:728`): a part that has to move alone has to be its own mesh.

**Apply the pose as `model.transform`, and rebuild it every frame in `update`.** Set `model.transform = M` and draw at the origin with scale 1; `DrawModel` composes an identity onto it, so `M` passes through untouched. Keep the current pose of every node in one function, so there is exactly one place where a placement can be wrong.

**A moving part has no single bounding box.** Sample the pose over the cycle and union the eight transformed corners of the local box, as `SweepBounds` in `models/ak47.c:1902` does. Do not hand the job to raylib: `GetModelBoundingBox` transforms only the box's own min and max corner and carries the warning "does not support rotation transformations" (`vendor/raylib/src/rmodels.c:1243`), so a rotating part frames wrong under `--part`.

**Measure the constraint instead of asserting it.** A joint can be wrong without looking wrong in any single frame. `CheckHalfShaft` in `models/humvee.c:2377` walks the suspension travel and measures how far each shaft's outer end sits inside the hub carrier it drives, and it earned its place: the shafts were rigid to the chassis in the first draft and ended up 0.110 m clear of that carrier, which only a review caught. `models/humvee.c` runs seven such checks at build time (`models/humvee.c:2530`), and `CheckAction` in `models/ak47.c:1789` does the same for the rifle's travel, clearances and ejection. This is the same rule as the mesh claims in review.md: compute the answer, do not argue for it.

**Put brief events on a fraction of the cycle, so that any sensible `--frames` lands on them.** `--anim` samples N evenly spaced phases, so an event shorter than one interval is caught only by luck. `models/ak47.c` fires three rounds at phases 0, 0.2 and 0.4 of a five-shot-period cycle, which means any `--frames` that is a multiple of 5 lands a frame on every ignition and its 3 ms muzzle flash. Phase 0 is the first event for the same reason: it is the one phase every `--frames` value samples, and it is also what `--part` renders. Choose the cycle length to make that true rather than hoping.

**A longer cycle needs proportionally more check and sweep steps.** Going from one shot to a burst multiplied the real time in a cycle by five and silently coarsened every fixed-step loop by the same factor. `CheckAction` samples a case crossing 5.7 mm of receiver wall in about 3 ms and reports millimetre margins; the step count has to keep the sampling finer than the margin claimed, and `SweepBounds` has to be fine enough to land on a 6 ms dwell at full travel.

**A check must be written in the frame the thing it checks against lives in.** The moment a model gains a parent transform, every build-time check written in world coordinates silently becomes wrong: it compares a moved part against a limit that moved with it. Adding recoil to `models/ak47.c` made its trigger check report the shoe 21 mm through a trigger guard it never touches, because the guard recoils too. Transform back into the parent's frame (`MatrixInvert`) before comparing.

**Sampling an axis proves nothing about a body with thickness.** `models/ak47.c` checked its ejected case's centreline against the ejection port and reported a clean pass; the case is 11.35 mm thick against a 17 mm opening, and sampling its surface instead showed brass going through the plate. Two further defects were hidden the same way: a bound checked in one axis but not the other, and a clearance that only held because the extreme was never sampled. Sample the envelope, at a resolution finer than the margin you are claiming: a 16-point ring on a 5.7 mm radius resolves to 0.11 mm, so it can support a 1.2 mm margin and could not have supported the 0.1 mm one an 8-point ring first reported.

**Playback speed is one constant, and it is not the geometry.** `models/ak47.c` keeps `SLOWDOWN` as the only quantity about playback rather than about the rifle, and `CYCLE` is `TOTAL_T * SLOWDOWN`. It scales the interactive window and `SCENE.duration` and nothing else, since `--anim` samples N evenly spaced phases whatever it is set to. A model can equally derive everything from its cycle instead, as `models/humvee.c` does by computing `SPEED` from `CYCLE`, in which case the cycle is already real time. Either is fine; say which.

Watch for constants that couple to it. `models/ak47.c` holds a muzzle flash for a minimum of `FLASH_MIN_PLAYBACK` of playback time so a 60 Hz window cannot drop a 3 ms event, and that floor is divided by `SLOWDOWN`, so changing the playback rate changes how many rendered frames a flash occupies. Count the affected frames before and after rather than assuming a playback constant cannot reach the renders.

**Skinning is not available.** `UpdateModelAnimationBones` needs bone attributes the harness's shader does not declare (`vendor/raylib/examples/shaders/resources/shaders/glsl330/lighting.vs` has only position, texcoord, normal and colour), and the CPU path `UpdateModelAnimation` still needs `boneIds`/`boneWeights` that no builder here emits. So a deforming surface cannot be posed at all, and a continuous loft split at a joint will simply come apart. Rigid nodes are the only option.
